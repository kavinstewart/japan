    釣船茶屋ざうお · Zauo

    You catch it.
    They cook it.
    Everybody screams.

    Dinner is sitting on a giant wooden boat over a fish tank, holding a fishing rod. Whatever you hook goes straight to the kitchen and comes back as sashimi, tempura or grilled with salt, cooked how you like.

    How it goes down

    1. Grab a rod, pick a target

      The tank is full of sea bream, flounder, horse mackerel and more. Each fish has a lower "you caught it" price and a higher "just order it" price, so catching is cheaper.

    2. Hook it

      Staff help the little ones and scoop with a net.

    3. The drum

      When you land one, staff bang a drum and the room cheers. It's real, and it's loud.

    4. Choose how it's cooked

      Sashimi, salt-grilled, simmered, tempura or butter-grilled. Leftover bones can come back as soup.

    Practice the celebration

    For the hype. Sound on.

    The one rule: there's no catch and release.If it's on your hook, it's on your bill. Agree on a fish budget before handing nine people rods.

    What's swimming

    • Sea bream tai, the classic
    • Flounder hirame
    • Horse mackerel aji, great as tempura
    • Spiny lobster for the brave wallet
    • Turban shell sazae
    • Abalone & shrimp varies by day

    Cook it how?

    • Sashimi
    • Salt-grilled
    • Simmered
    • Tempura / fried
    • Butter-grilled

    ⚡ Mission: Pokémon Center

    The nearest one is a walk across the river

    Pokémon Center Skytree Town is inside the Tokyo Solamachi mall at the foot of Tokyo Skytree. You can see it from the hotel.

    Pokémon Center Skytree Town

    Tokyo Solamachi, East Yard 4F · 1-1-2 Oshiage, Sumida · open about 10am–9pm

    • ~20 minWalk it: over the river bridge, along Mizumachi and up to Skytree.
    • 1 stopTobu Skytree Line: Asakusa to Tokyo Skytree Station.
    • 2 stopsToei Asakusa Line: Asakusa to Oshiage.

    The walk, step by step

    1. From the hotel, head to the river. The Sumida River Walk footbridge runs right beside the train bridge, and parts of the floor are glass.
    2. Cross, then follow Tokyo Mizumachi, a row of shops and cafés under the train tracks.
    3. Walk toward the giant tower; you can't miss it. Go into Solamachi, take the escalators up to East Yard, 4th floor.
    4. Look for the store's mascot, Rayquaza, and the Skytree-only merch.

    Level up: more Pokémon

    PokéPark KANTO big one

    A permanent Pokémon park at Yomiuriland that opened Feb 2026. There's no gate or same-day sales; tickets go on sale online in blocks at 8pm Japan time. About 30–40 min from Shinjuku. Check tickets now

    Pokémon Café, Nihonbashi reservation only

    You can't get in without a booking. Slots open 31 days ahead at 6pm Japan time and go in minutes, so keep refreshing for cancellations. The giant Pokémon Center Tokyo DX is next door. Reserve

    Shibuya PARCO 6F, home of the life-size Mewtwo. Pair it with a Shibuya Crossing stop.

    Poké Lids in Ueno Park

    Pokémon manhole covers near the science museum and the Tokyo National Museum. Ueno is 5 min from Asakusa on the Ginza Line, and it's where the shinkansen to Echigo-Yuzawa leaves from.

    Reopened in Aug 2026 with entry restrictions. Check how entry works before you go.

    Mochi's surprise box

    Stuff you didn't know you needed

    Ideas for the open days. Most are within walking distance, and one is a spaceship.

    🚀

    Take the spaceship boat to Odaiba

    Tokyo Cruise runs sci-fi water buses from Asakusa pier. Hotaluna goes to Hinode in about 40 min (¥1,400, kids ¥700) and Emeraldas to Odaiba in about 55 min. They're glass bubbles on a river.

    Hotaluna skips Oct 20–21 and Emeraldas skips Oct 27. The relaunched Tokyo Mizube Line also runs Asakusa to Odaiba from Oct 17, Wed–Sun. Tokyo Cruise timetable

    🎢

    Hanayashiki

    Japan's oldest amusement park, 5 min from the hotel, with a roller coaster that's been running since 1953. It's small, retro and perfect for a jet-lagged afternoon.

    10am–6pm · entry ¥1,600 · adult ride pass ¥4,600

    🍤

    Make your own fake food

    Kappabashi is a whole street of restaurant-supply shops, including the plastic food in Tokyo's restaurant windows. At Ganso Shokuhin Sample-ya the kids make wax tempura and lettuce in about 40 minutes.

    Booking required. It's taught in Japanese, but it's very show-and-copy. Look for the giant chef's head on the Niimi building.

    ~13 min walk · roughly ¥3,000+ per person

    🍵

    The matcha level 7 challenge

    Suzukien sells matcha gelato in seven strengths. Everyone starts at level 1. Who can take No. 7?

    10am–5pm · closed Wed Oct 21 · ~6 min walk

    🐱

    Mochi's hometown

    Imado Shrine says it's where the maneki-neko, the beckoning lucky cat, was born. It's full of cat statues, and sometimes a real cat too.

    ~12 min walk up the river

    🐄

    Pet the lucky cow

    Ushijima Shrine has a stone cow called nade-ushi. Rub the part of the cow where you hurt and it's supposed to heal. Good after a 10-hour flight.

    Across the river, on the Pokémon walk

    🌅

    Free rooftop view

    The Asakusa Culture Tourist Information Center, opposite Kaminarimon, has a free 8th-floor terrace that looks down Nakamise to Senso-ji. Ekimise's rooftop does Skytree plus trains.

    🍁

    Echigo-Yuzawa: the day-trip game plan (Sat 10/24)

    Morning: shinkansen from Ueno, about 70 min to snow country. Midday: the Yuzawa Kogen Ropeway; its Autumn Fair runs to Nov 8, and late October is around peak autumn colour. Up top there's a zipline, a summer bobsled and a café. Before the train home: Ponshukan inside the station. Adults get 5 sake tastings from coin machines for ¥500. Everyone can do the sake bath onsen and giant bakudan rice balls.

    GALA Yuzawa is closed until ski season. Ropeway runs 8:40am–4:40pm and can stop for weather. Ropeway info

    ♨️

    Footbath after teamLab

    Toyosu Senkyaku Banrai is an Edo-style food hall one stop from teamLab, with a free footbath and a bay view. Wet feet, warm feet.

    🏮

    Nakamise after dark

    When the shops close in the evening, the shutters come down and show painted scenes of old Asakusa. You'll have the street almost to yourselves.

    Getting from Narita to the hotel

    • The Keisei Access Express runs straight to Asakusa Station with no transfers, in about an hour (about ¥1,310). Board trains bound for Haneda Airport or Nishi-Magome; Keisei-Ueno trains don't stop at Asakusa.
    • The hotel is about 3 min from Asakusa Station.
    • With nine people, bags and sleepy kids at 9pm, a pre-booked van is worth pricing.

    Money & trains

    • Suica in Apple Wallet works on every train, at konbini and at vending machines, and you can top it up with a US card.
    • Under-6s ride trains free with an adult. Older kids pay child fares, but a phone Suica charges adult fare.
    • 7-Eleven ATMs take foreign cards. Some small shops and shrines are still cash only.
    • No tipping, ever. It can confuse people.

    Manners Mochi cares about

    • Nakamise: don't eat while walking. Eat in front of the stall you bought from, then move on.
    • Trash cans are rare, so carry a little bag for wrappers.
    • Trains are quiet: no phone calls, and keep voices low.
    • Shoes come off wherever you see a step up or slippers.

    Weather & packing

    • Late October is roughly mid-60s °F days and mid-50s nights. Pack layers.
    • Bring a rain jacket; October gets real rain.
    • teamLab: shorts or pants that roll above the knee. You wade barefoot and some floors are mirrors.
    • Comfy shoes. You'll walk 15,000 steps without noticing.
